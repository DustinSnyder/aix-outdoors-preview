Project tools

Four quick calculators for planning a job before you call. Every number here is a planning estimate — not a bid — and the assumptions are written out so you can sanity-check them yourself.

These calculators run in your browser only — nothing is sent anywhere, and nothing here is a quote. Use Request a free estimate once you have real numbers to work from.

Gravel / stone volume

Figure out roughly how many tons of crushed stone a lane or pad needs before you call the quarry.

—

Assumption: crushed limestone at roughly 1.4 tons per cubic yard — your quarry’s stone may run lighter or heavier, so ask for their number if you have it. This is length × width × depth, converted to cubic yards, then to tons. Plan to order about 20% extra to cover compaction once it’s spread and packed. Planning estimate only — not a bid.

Acreage from a rectangle

Useful for sizing a mowing job, a clearing pass, or a food plot when you only have a tape measure or a GPS distance.

—

One acre is 43,560 square feet. This assumes a simple rectangle — an irregular field will need to be broken into a few rectangles and added together, or measured with a GPS app. Good for a first pass on mowing, clearing, or plot sizing before a walk-through.

Food plot seed

Turn a seed-rate number (from the bag or your plan) into a total pounds figure for the plot you actually have.

—

Seeding rate varies a lot by species and mix — a straight brassica, a cereal grain, and a clover blend are not the same number. Use the rate printed on your seed tag or plan; this just multiplies it by your acreage. Not sized to soil test results.

Trail length

Convert a corridor length in feet (paced off, GPS-tracked, or measured) into miles for planning a walk or a machine day.

—

One mile is 5,280 feet. Handy for turning a paced-off or GPS trail length into a figure that’s easier to talk about — machine time on trail work is still priced by the linear foot, not the mile.
