FAQ

Short answers so the quote form is not doing all the work.

What drives the cost?

Acreage, density, terrain, access, stone index, and the finish you want (mulch in place, plot, trail, driveway). Sample ranges on this site are for orientation only — not a bid.

How long does a job take?

A small fence-line or stand-lane day can finish in a weather window. Multi-acre clearing, plots, or a long gravel lift take more crew days. The quote names a start week.

Do you haul brush away?

Default on clearing is mulching in place as a natural mulch layer. Haul-off is a separate catalog line — dump-truck time, plus tipping as a pass-through — when you do not want that layer left. A burn pile is only if you ask and permit it.

Can you work steep or wet ground?

Tracked machines, and remote-capable gear where the bank or wet draw needs it. If we cannot reach it without leaving ruts you will hate in April, we say so in the quote.

Do you work Cedar Rapids?

No. Cedar Rapids sits outside the 100-mile working radius of Burlington 52601. Iowa City, the Quad Cities, Hannibal, and Quincy are treated as edge jobs and depend on the day’s routing.

Is the pricing on this site a bid?

No. Every figure is a sample range for this design. A bid would be a dated document with acres, linear feet, stone index, and a finish condition.

Will there be an owner photo or bio?

Not in this brand system. AIX Outdoors is presented as a land crew and a radius, not a personality site.

Do you sell hunting stands or seed retail?

No. Stand management is lane, approach, and pad work on equipment you already own. Plot seed is specified in the install, not sold from a shop counter in this sample.

When do you haul instead of mulching in place?

Mulch in place is the default finish on clearing. We haul when you cannot dump on site, when brush or spoil has to leave, or when a driveway needs stone delivered. Same 100-mile radius of Burlington 52601. Sample from $165 / hour dump-body time — not a bid.

Can you combine services in one mobilization?

Yes — that is the usual quote. Clearing into a plot, trails plus stand lanes, or driveway resurfacing plus stone haul, saves a second farm visit.

What about permits and burns?

Burn windows, county brush rules, and anything touching a public right-of-way stay with the landowner in this sample scope.

How do I know if I am inside 100 miles?

Use the service-area map. If you are on the list, ask. If you are Cedar Rapids, we will decline.

What drives the price on a real job?

Acreage or linear feet, stem/brush density, terrain (slope, wet ground, rock), how the crew and equipment get access, and haul distance if material has to move. Two five-acre clearing jobs can price differently if one is flat old pasture brush and the other is a steep bank thick with locust. Sample ranges on this site are for orientation only — a real quote names the actual drivers.

How long does a typical job take?

A stand lane or a short fence-line pass can finish inside one weather window. Multi-acre clearing, a full food-plot install, or a long gravel lift run several crew days. The quote names a start week and a rough duration, not a vibe.

Do you haul material away, or mulch in place?

Mulching in place is the default finish on clearing — it becomes a natural mulch layer, not a pile you have to deal with. Haul-off is a separate line: dump-truck time plus tipping as a pass-through, for when you specifically do not want that layer left, or when a job (like a driveway lift) needs material brought in instead.

Do you work in winter or on wet ground?

Some of it, yes — tracked, remote-capable equipment can handle frozen ground and some wet draws that a wheeled machine can’t. But if the ground is genuinely too soft or too frozen to work without leaving ruts you’ll hate in April, we say so and reschedule instead of running equipment anyway. Winter can actually be a good window for clearing, once brush is easier to see and ground firms up.

How does scheduling and a deposit work?

If a job needs material ordered ahead of time (seed, stone), a separate materials deposit may be requested at order time. Moving a scheduled date requires reasonable advance notice (illustratively, 5 business days) so the slot can be rebooked; weather-driven reschedules are handled without penalty. What happens if you find extra work once you are on site?

We stop and talk before we do it. If the scope changes once equipment is on the ground — more brush than the photos showed, a culvert that’s worse than expected, extra material needed — that becomes a new quote for the added scope, not a surprise line on the invoice. You approve it before we proceed.

Are estimates given in writing?

Yes. A real quote is a dated document naming acres or linear feet, access notes, the finish condition, and a price — not a verbal number. Everything labeled “sample” on this site is for design review only and is not that document.

Are you insured?

SAMPLE policy placeholder. As drafted: general liability coverage appropriate to land-clearing and grading work, with a certificate available on request before a job starts. The owner should confirm actual coverage and carrier details before this claim goes live.

Will you travel outside the 100-mile radius?

Not by default — the working radius in this sample is 100 miles of Burlington, IA 52601, and Cedar Rapids-type requests outside it are declined rather than quoted with a hidden travel charge. Edge-of-radius properties are considered case by case, and if we do take one, a travel line is written into the quote instead of folded into the price.
