What does land clearing cost?

Land clearing has a minimum flat rate of $2,000 per day. That is a floor, not a package deal. Each job quote is unique depending on what you want accomplished — density, access, terrain, and finish goals all change the number. Call or request a free estimate with photos.

What is your approach to invasive plant clearing?

We remove aggressive invasive species common across Iowa — multiflora rose, bush honeysuckle, autumn olive, and other brush that spreads quickly and reduces forage and cover for wildlife. Forestry mulching finishes the work in place. Our approach prioritizes efficient clearing while protecting desirable trees and understory so native habitat recovers stronger. Send photos of the thicket and we will tell you if a mulching pass is the right tool.

What is timber stand improvement (TSI)?

Timber stand improvement is selective thinning — removing competing or low-value stems and opening the canopy to favor higher-quality hardwoods and better wildlife habitat. Whether the goal is healthier timber, improved hunting cover, or cleaner, more usable woods, we tailor TSI to the property and the landowner’s objectives. Hunting stand lanes can be part of the same visit when you need them.

Do you clear honeysuckle, autumn olive, buckthorn, and other invasives?

Yes. Invasive plant clearing and forestry mulching on private land often targets bush honeysuckle, autumn olive, multiflora rose, buckthorn, thorny honey locust thickets, Callery pear, oriental bittersweet, and similar woody problems common in southeast Iowa. Send photos and we will tell you if mulching is the right tool.

Do you haul brush away?

Default on clearing is mulching in place as a natural mulch layer. Haul-off is a separate catalog line — dump-truck time, plus tipping as a pass-through — when you do not want that layer left. A burn pile is only if you ask and permit it.

What drives the price on a real job?

Acreage or linear feet, stem/brush density, terrain (slope, wet ground, rock), how the crew and equipment get access, and haul distance if material has to move. Two five-acre clearing jobs can price differently if one is flat old pasture brush and the other is a steep bank thick with locust. Sample ranges on this site are for orientation only — a real quote names the actual drivers.

How long does a typical job take?

A stand lane or a short fence-line pass can finish inside one weather window. Multi-acre clearing, a full food-plot install, or a long gravel lift run several crew days. The quote names a start week and a rough duration, not a vibe.

Do you haul material away, or mulch in place?

Mulching in place is the default finish on clearing — it becomes a natural mulch layer, not a pile you have to deal with. Haul-off is a separate line: dump-truck time plus tipping as a pass-through, for when you specifically do not want that layer left, or when a job (like a driveway lift) needs material brought in instead.

Do you work in winter or on wet ground?

Some of it, yes — tracked, remote-capable equipment can handle frozen ground and some wet draws that a wheeled machine can’t. But if the ground is genuinely too soft or too frozen to work without leaving ruts you’ll hate in April, we say so and reschedule instead of running equipment anyway. Winter can actually be a good window for clearing, once brush is easier to see and ground firms up.
