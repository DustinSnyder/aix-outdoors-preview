More than “just mow it”

Mowing and mulching are different tools for different growth — using the wrong one wastes a trip.

A rotary or batwing mower knocks back grass, forbs, and small stems in one pass and is priced by the acre. Once brush and saplings get past a certain stem diameter, mowing just bounces off them instead of cutting them, and a forestry mulching head is the right tool instead. We also set the height left on purpose — a wildlife-cover cut and a clean hay-field look are different requests with different blade settings.

When mowing is the wrong tool

Brush and saplings beyond mowing size need to move to the clearing line, not get mowed twice and called done. We’ll say that on the walk-through so you’re not paying for a pass that won’t hold.

Good to know

When to mow and when to mulch

Mowing and mulching solve the same general problem — ground taken over by growth — but they fit different stages. A rotary or batwing mower is fast and cheaper per acre on grass, forbs, and stems it can shear: CRP-style fields, plot perimeters, roadsides. It resets growth that will need cutting again; it does not kill woody brush permanently.

Once brush and saplings get past a certain stem size, a mower stops cutting and starts bouncing. That is when forestry mulching is the right tool — multiflora rose, honeysuckle, cedar, sapling thickets. It costs more per acre than mowing, but it finishes the job. A field let go for years often needs both: mulch first, then a mowing schedule to keep it from getting there again. We say so before running the wrong machine.
