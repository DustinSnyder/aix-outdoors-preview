Hunting Feeding Plots

Installed plots with a soil number, a seed plan, and a first mow — sized to the farm, not a social post.

We do not drop a mix on sod and call it a plot. Typical install: soil test, lime/fert as indicated, kill or till, drill or broadcast, pack, and a 30-day check. Brassica, cereal, clover, and mixed hides are all in play — you pick the hunt calendar, we pick the rate.

  • ½-acre kill plots up to multi-acre destination fields
  • Screening cover on the approach if the field is naked

Sound familiar?

A plot that doesn’t hunt is just a garden.

  • Plot too big and too bright — deer stage on the edge at dusk and never step in
  • Wrong wind for the one stand that’s supposed to cover it
  • Poor soil pH nobody tested before the seed went down
  • Weeds and volunteer grass that took the plot over by August
  • No screening cover on the approach, so every deer sees you before you see them

More than seed on dirt

A plot is habitat, not a seed-bag photo.

A soil test, lime or fertilizer as indicated, a real seedbed (kill or till, then pack), and a species matched to your hunt calendar and the stand’s wind is what makes a plot perform. Broadcasting a mix on unprepped sod and hoping is what makes a plot fail by mid-summer. Shape and size matter as much as seed — a half-acre kill plot and a multi-acre destination field are different designs, not the same job at different scales.

When the spot is the problem, not the seed

Heavy shade, poor drainage, or a location with no realistic wind for a stand means the plot won’t perform no matter what goes in the ground. We’ll tell you the spot is wrong before we let you blame the mix.
