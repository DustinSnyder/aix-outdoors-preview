Trail Creation and Management

New cuts and yearly refresh so you can move stands, cameras, and a quiet cart without advertising it to the ridge.

Sound familiar?

A trail that erodes was never a trail — it was a suggestion.

  • No quiet way onto the property without walking a road ditch
  • Mud holes that swallow a boot every wet spring
  • Ruts deep enough to twist an ankle in the dark on the walk in
  • A cut that runs straight down a slope instead of angling across it, so every rain carves it deeper
  • Blowdown blocking the line every time the wind picks up

More than a mowed line

A trail is designed to the contour so it survives — not cut wherever it was easiest.

Laying the line to the topography, with turnouts and simple water bars where a draw crosses it, is what keeps a trail from becoming a ditch after one wet season. A straight line cut for convenience does the opposite: water finds it, follows it, and carves it out. We also scope width on purpose — hunter-quiet and narrow reads different than a UTV-width service road, and the machine time is priced accordingly.

When a straight line is the wrong call

Steep grades or wet bottoms sometimes mean the trail has to switchback, cross on stone, or simply not go where you first pictured it. We’ll say that on the walk-through, before we cut it, instead of after it washes out.
